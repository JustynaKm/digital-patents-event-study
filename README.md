# digital-patents-event-study
Data pipeline and empirical event study of US digital vs. traditional patents, analyzing patent characteristics and stock market reactions to guide R&amp;D and IP value creation

This study uses the publicly available KPSS Extended Data repository (KPSS2017/Technological-Innovation-Resource-Allocation-and-Growth-Extended-Data, updated through 2025) as its empirical baseline. The baseline pipeline relies on three core datasets covering 1926–2025:KPSS_2025.csv — Patent-level panel data (1926–2025) containing patent identifiers (patent_num), application filing dates (filing_date), grant/issue dates (issue_date), nominal patent value estimates (xi_nominal, in millions of nominal dollars), CPI-deflated real patent value estimates (xi_real, in 1982 million dollars), and forward citations (cites).Match_patent_permco_permno_2025.csv — Patent-to-CRSP firm matching table (permco and permno) linking granted patents to U.S. public equities; patents matched to multiple CRSP permnos are excluded in the KPSS construction.Match_patent_cpc_2025.csv — Patent-to-Cooperative Patent Classification (cpc) matching table linking patents to technology classes (noting that 3,549 patents through 2025 have missing CPC information).(Note: Updated KPSS series incorporate post-QJE refinements—including updated CRSP coverage, matching improvements, parameter re-estimation such as $\gamma = 0.0155$ in the 2025 sample, and 52-week trailing return volatility windows—and are supplemented locally with USPTO bulk/metadata records for patent descriptions and attributes.)3.3 Classifying Digital-Intensive vs. Traditional PatentsA central methodological question in this thesis is how to rigorously distinguish digital-intensive patents (spanning software, computing, networking, and AI/data technologies) from traditional patents. In the empirical pipeline, patent segmentation combines:Technological classification (cpc): Grouping patents using Cooperative Patent Classification codes from Match_patent_cpc_2025.csv (alongside patent scope/diversity across CPC classes) to separate digital/computing/communications classes from traditional physical or industrial classes.USPTO metadata & text enrichment: Merging patent-level USPTO records (such as titles, abstracts, claims, and long-text fields) to cross-check keyword/semantic signals for software, AI, and digital architectures against CPC-based classifications.
